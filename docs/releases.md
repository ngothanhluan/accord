# Release notes

Each section lists what changes for tickets in flight and for repositories pinned to an older accord. A release pull request copies the `## Unreleased` section into its `release:` commit body and renames the section to the release version.

## Unreleased

Every rule below has a cut-off constant in `packages/core/src/round/pin.ts`, and each one holds the first release that ships the rule. The constants read `0.4.1` while `v0.4.1` is not yet tagged.

- Discuss stage (from `DISCUSS_FROM`, 0.4.1): a ticket of lane M, L or P (every lane but S) claimed under that pin or later needs `accord/tickets/<id>/discuss.md`, with every gray area mapped to a Decision, before `accord gate ready` passes. The areas are the list items at the top level of a line (indented by up to three spaces; four spaces or a tab make a note under an area) under the `## Gray areas` heading and under any heading that starts with `Gray areas`, such as `## Gray areas (cont.)`. A repeated or continued heading, and a line with a group and a Decision but no gray area text, fail the gate. A ticket claimed under an older pin, in flight when the repository upgrades, needs none.
- Approach critic (from `APPROACH_CAP_FROM`, 0.4.1): for a ticket of lane M, L or P the critic runs at most two rounds, and no Ruling raises the cap. A third round is refused with the findings still open. An open `risk: data-loss` or `risk: security` finding blocks the approval, in every round of such a ticket and when round 2 reports no findings or a verdict ends the loop: `accord show <id> --approval --short` lists it under `## Blocking findings`, and `accord approve` refuses with exit 1 and names it. This is a change in public behaviour: `accord approve` read no approach record before 0.4.1. A risk finding is open, whatever its severity, while its state is `open`, `unverified` or `deferred` (a risk finding is never deferred), while it is `repeated as` a finding that is not in a later round of the cycle, and while an `owner:` state has no words. A finding repeated as another, or repeated by a `repeats <round>.<n>:` line, passes its risk on, so a repeat line without a `risk:` mark blocks too. A risk finding stops blocking when it is `fixed`, `withdrawn`, repeated as a finding that exists, or dropped by the owner as `owner: "<words>"` (the short view then lists it under `## Risks` with the owner's words), and when any `## Closed` line (the owner's no with a Correction, `ruled out by`, `the owner picked`) starts a new cycle. A ticket claimed under an older pin keeps five rounds and its stop, even when its approach critique begins after the upgrade, so no round is lost; `accord approve` does not refuse it, and the short view lists its open risk findings under `## Risks`.
- Approval (from 0.4.1, a skill change shipped by `accord upgrade`): `accord-intent` asks no separate yes. The owner approves once, from `accord show <id> --approval --short`, which prints the Intent, one line per criterion, the Decisions, the risks and the ticket path. `accord show <id> --approval` prints the full sections as before.
- Repositories pinned to 0.4.0: a repository pinned to 0.4.0 keeps the 0.4.0 answers and skills until `accord upgrade`, which moves the pin and regenerates the skills. Every Phase 3 rule reads the pin at the ticket's claim commit: the newest `claim: <id>` commit that is not on the default branch, else the newest one overall. A ticket with no `claim: <id>` commit has no claim commit to read, so it gets the new rules after the upgrade, wherever its ticket file was added. It reads the working-tree pin only when the ticket has no such commit or git has no HEAD. When git fails reading the pin, or `accord/config.yml` cannot be read at the claim commit (a blob a blobless clone never fetched), the new rules apply. Then `accord gate ready` names the git error in its discuss finding, `accord round --approach` names it in the refusals the cap rules give (past the cap, an ended loop, a blocked approval), `accord approve` names it when it refuses on a blocking finding, and `accord show <id> --approval --short` names it in its `## Blocking findings` part; the other rules that read the pin apply the new rules without naming it. So a ticket claimed before the upgrade keeps every old rule until it is done and the upgrade changes nothing for it.
