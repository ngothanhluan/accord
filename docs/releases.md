# Release notes

Each section lists what changes for tickets in flight and for repositories pinned to an older accord. A release pull request copies the `## Unreleased` section into its `release:` commit body and renames the section to the release version.

## Unreleased

Each rule below that reads a pin has a cut-off constant in `packages/core/src/round/pin.ts` (`DISCUSS_FROM`, `APPROACH_CAP_FROM`, `PLAN_FORMAT_FROM`, `PLAN_CAP_FROM`, `PLAN_GROUNDS_FROM`, `GROUNDS_V2_FROM` and `PLAN_SIZE_FROM`), which holds the first release that ships the rule. The seven read `0.4.1` while `v0.4.1` is not yet tagged.

- Discuss stage (from `DISCUSS_FROM`, 0.4.1): a ticket of lane M, L or P (every lane but S) claimed under that pin or later needs `accord/tickets/<id>/discuss.md`, with every gray area mapped to a Decision, before `accord gate ready` passes. The areas are the list items at the top level of a line (indented by up to three spaces and less than the text of the area before it: a sub-bullet indented as far as that text, two spaces under `- ` or three under `1. `, and any item indented four spaces or by a tab, is a note under the area; a paragraph after a blank line ends the list, so an item after it is top-level again) under the `## Gray areas` heading and under any heading that starts with `Gray areas`, such as `## Gray areas (cont.)`. A repeated or continued heading, and a line with a group and a Decision but no gray area text, fail the gate. A ticket claimed under an older pin, in flight when the repository upgrades, needs none.
- Approach critic (from `APPROACH_CAP_FROM`, 0.4.1): for a ticket of lane M, L or P the critic runs at most two rounds, and no Ruling raises the cap. A third round is refused with the findings still open. An open `risk: data-loss` or `risk: security` finding blocks the approval, in every round of such a ticket and when round 2 reports no findings or a verdict ends the loop: `accord show <id> --approval --short` lists it under `## Blocking findings`, and `accord approve` refuses with exit 1 and names it. This is a change in public behaviour: `accord approve` read no approach record before 0.4.1. The approach record is read strictly and fails closed. The one separator is ` — `; a hyphen, two hyphens or an en dash is part of the text of a state, a reason, a test or a command, so `command: npm test -- <path>` is never cut. A risk is read only from the finding's mark slot: a ` — `-separated segment after the finding's text that is `risk: security` or `risk: data-loss`, or `rests on: risk: security` or `rests on: risk: data-loss` (the critic's own wording), in any letter case, after the state or before it, whether the severity is followed by ` — `, a colon, a hyphen or an en dash (`1. Must: <text> — risk: security — open` is read like `1. Must — <text> — risk: security — open`); or the `rests on:` line of the critic's block, the lines under a finding that are indented deeper than it, as `agents/critic.md` prints it. The block's other `rests on:` lines (`ac-<n>`, `D-<nn>`, a quote, `test:`, `command:`) are read as grounds, so a round 2 block with `rests on: ac-1` is not refused for grounds; a block may carry several of them, and a ground on the finding line may stand beside one in the block, and every one is read: a second ground, or a second `test:` or `command:`, is never a record problem. Any other line under a finding, indented or not, is a note, and a note is prose: `   - Risk: a stale cache shows old rows` refuses nothing. Prose is never searched for a risk word: the finding's own words, a note under a `## Round` heading, the reason of a `withdrawn:` or `unverified:` state, the owner's words, a `No findings` line and a test name such as `risk:data-loss` are read as nothing, so they never refuse. A finding that names a risk only in prose, or in a line with no ` — `, is not a blocking finding; while it is an open Must or Should the short view lists it under `## Risks`, and an open Nice is never listed. A mark slot is a segment after the finding's text, or a `rests on` line of the block, whose key is `risk`, `risks` or `rests on` and whose value is a security or data-loss kind written some way, and a mark slot whose value does not parse is a record problem: `risks: security`, `risk: data_loss`, `risk; security`, `risk, security`, a mark in backticks, quotes or asterisks, `risk: security.`, a hyphen that is not the ASCII one as in `data‑loss`, a kind one edit off the whole word, counted over its letters, the `data` part of `data-loss` included, and whatever up to three characters that are no letter separate the letters or come before them (`risk: secrity`, `risk: date-loss`, `risk: data-lost`, `risk: data/loss`, `risk: secu-rity`, `risk: .security`; a value two or more letters off is prose) and `rests on risk: security` (no colon) are problems. A value that is no such kind is prose, not a problem: `risk: none`, `risk: low`, `risk: a partial file is left on a crash` and `rests on: ac1` refuse nothing. A line directly under a finding, with no blank line between, that is no deeper than the finding and is a mark slot (`rests on: risk: security` or `risk: security` at the start of the next line) is a record problem too, because Markdown renders it as part of the finding: indent it under the finding, or leave a blank line before it, so that it is a note. Every cycle of the record is held to this, and any other line of `approach-review.md` that accord cannot read is a record problem as well: `accord round --approach` refuses it, `accord approve` refuses with exit 1 naming the problems, and the short view lists them under `## Blocking findings`. The plan record of a lane M, L or P ticket claimed under 0.4.1 or later follows the grounds rule from its first round, whatever pin its rounds were written under (see the plan checker's grounds below), so the same record problem refuses `accord round --plan` and `accord gate plan` (rule `gate.plan-rounds`) from round 1. For a ticket claimed under an older pin, a plan record follows the grounds rule only when its first round is written under 0.4.1 or later, and then the problem refuses `accord round --plan` and, once a cycle holds three rounds, `accord gate plan`, as before; a plan record of such a ticket begun earlier is read as before. `accord gate done` reads no plan record. A ticket claimed under an older pin and a lane S ticket are asked none of this: `accord approve` still reads no record for them. A risk finding is open, whatever its severity, while its state is `open`, `unverified` or `deferred` (a risk finding is never deferred), while it is `repeated as` a finding that is not in a later round of the cycle, and while an `owner:` state has no words. A finding repeated as another, or repeated by a `repeats <round>.<n>:` line, passes its risk on, so a repeat line without a `risk:` mark blocks too. A risk finding stops blocking when it is `fixed`, `withdrawn`, repeated as a finding that exists, or dropped by the owner as `owner: "<words>"` (the short view then lists it under `## Risks` with the owner's words), and when any `## Closed` line (the owner's no with a Correction, `ruled out by`, `the owner picked`) starts a new cycle. A ticket claimed under an older pin keeps five rounds and its stop, even when its approach critique begins after the upgrade, so no round is lost; `accord approve` does not refuse it, and the short view lists its open risk findings under `## Risks`.
- Approval (from 0.4.1, a skill change shipped by `accord upgrade`): `accord-intent` asks no separate yes. The owner approves once, from `accord show <id> --approval --short`, which prints the Intent, one line per criterion, the Decisions, the risks and the ticket path. `accord show <id> --approval` prints the full sections as before.
- Plan format (from `PLAN_FORMAT_FROM`, 0.4.1): for a ticket of lane M, L or P, `accord gate plan` reads each task of `plan.md` as `accord brief` prints it (an HTML comment hides nothing) and refuses three things, one finding per line. A task with no `after:` line, or whose first `after:` line holds nothing, is refused (`gate.task-after-missing`); the line names the tasks the task follows (`after: 1, 2`) or says `after: none`, and the key is read in lowercase only, as `covers:`, `files:` and `verify:` are, so `After:` or `after :` is not read and the finding names the line it skipped. A token of an `after:` line that is not a task of the plan, `none` written beside a task number included, is refused (`gate.task-after-unknown`); a task that names itself or a cycle of tasks is not checked here. A task holds code only as an interfaces block of one form: a line of three backticks and `interfaces` at column 0 opens it, and the first following line of three backticks at column 0 closes it, either line may end in spaces or tabs and holds nothing else, and a task may hold several such blocks. Every other fence-like line of a task, one that after any leading spaces, tabs, `>` or list markers starts with three or more backticks or tildes, is refused at its own line (`gate.task-code-block`): an indented fence, a fence on a list marker or in a quote, `~~~`, a longer run, a fence with a language such as `ts`, a code span at the start of a line, a fence inside an interfaces block, and an interfaces block no line closes, named at its opening line. A task of more than 120 non-blank lines is refused (`gate.task-too-long`); comment lines and interfaces blocks count. At the commit before this release the cap refuses 12 of 322 tasks in 11 of 49 plans of this repository (`docs/benchmark/metrics-st-ln-91-plan-format.md`). A lane S ticket is asked none of this. A ticket claimed under an older pin, in flight at the upgrade, passes as before even when its plan is written after the upgrade.
- Plan cap (from `PLAN_CAP_FROM`, 0.4.1): the plan critique runs at most three rounds per cycle, and stops the loop for the owner after a round from round 2 on whose count of Must and Should findings is above 0 and did not fall below the round before (a Nice and a finding deferred with its ledger line are not counted), as well as after a round from round 3 that still reports a Should or a Must. `accord round <id> --plan` prints `Round: <n> of 3`, gives the count in its stop reason, and refuses a fourth round; `accord gate plan` refuses a round past the cap or after the stop that no owner Ruling allows (`gate.plan-rounds`), and a stopped loop needs `verdict: accepted-by-owner "<the owner's sentence>"` after the stopping round, or a round the owner allows that reports no findings. An owner Ruling that allows a round still raises the cap. A ticket claimed under an older pin keeps five rounds and the stop from round 3, even when its plan critique begins after the upgrade.
- Plan checker (from `PLAN_CAP_FROM`, 0.4.1, a skill change shipped by `accord upgrade`): round 1 of each plan critique cycle dispatches one plan checker, the critic in plan mode with no lens running every plan check, plus one security critic when the ticket touches auth, money, migration or data deletion, or the plan touches a path under `sensitive:` in `accord/config.yml`. `accord round <id> --plan` prints `Critics: plan checker` at round 1 to say so, and `accord-plan` dispatches by that line. A ticket claimed under an older pin gets no such line and keeps the 0.4.0 round-1 split, one critic for a plan of one to three tasks and two critics for a plan of four or more tasks, in every cycle, with its five-round cap, now written in `accord-plan/legacy-round1.md` alone. There is one deliberate change for it: its security critic also runs when the plan touches a path under `sensitive:`, where 0.4.0 asked only about auth, money, migration and data deletion, so no security check is cut; nothing else changes in its plan critique.
- Plan checker's grounds (from `PLAN_GROUNDS_FROM`, 0.4.1): for a ticket of lane M, L or P, a Must or Should of plan critique round 1 that cites no ticket line (`rests on:` an `ac-<n>`, a `D-<nn>` or a quote), no `test:`, no `command:` and no `risk: data-loss` or `risk: security` stops `accord round <id> --plan`, which names it, until it is grounded or written `deferred` with its line and failing case under `## Deferred` in the ledger. A ticket claimed under an older pin keeps the Phase 2 rule: grounds from round 2, when the record's first round is written under 0.4.1 or later. The approach record keeps grounds from round 2 for every ticket.
- Repositories pinned to 0.4.0: a repository pinned to 0.4.0 keeps the 0.4.0 answers and skills until `accord upgrade`, which moves the pin and regenerates the skills. The discuss rule, the approach cap and the four plan rules above (format, cap, checker and round-1 grounds) read the pin at the ticket's claim commit: the newest `claim: <id>` commit that is not on the default branch, else the newest one overall. A ticket with no `claim: <id>` commit has no claim commit to read, so it gets the new rules after the upgrade, wherever its ticket file was added. It reads the working-tree pin only when the ticket has no such commit or git has no HEAD. When git fails reading the pin, or `accord/config.yml` cannot be read at the claim commit (a blob a blobless clone never fetched), the new rules apply. Then `accord gate ready` names the git error in its discuss finding, `accord round --approach` names it in the refusals the cap rules give (past the cap, an ended loop, a blocked approval), `accord approve` names it when it refuses on a blocking finding, and `accord show <id> --approval --short` names it in its `## Blocking findings` part; the cap itself (the round number and the cap it prints) applies the new rules without naming it. For the plan rules, every finding of `accord gate plan` under the format rules and `gate.plan-rounds`, and every answer of `accord round --plan` (the next round, as a `Note:` line, a stop and a refusal), adds the git error and `the claim pin could not be read, so the new plan rules apply`. Six rules follow the claim: the discuss rule, the approach cap with its approval block, the plan format, the plan cap with its count stop, the plan checker at round 1 and the plan's round-1 grounds, so a ticket claimed before the upgrade keeps no discuss stage, five approach rounds, the old plan format, five plan rounds and the 0.4.0 round-1 dispatch (with the security critic widened to `sensitive:` paths, the one deliberate change) until it is done. Two rules follow the record or the plan instead, and the upgrade changes them for such a ticket once a record or a plan begins after it: the grounds rule (round 2 and Must, `GROUNDS_V2_FROM`, and the plan fix scope) reads the pin at the first round of the record (`review.md`, `plan-review.md` or `approach-review.md`), except that the plan record of a ticket claimed under 0.4.1 or later is asked from round 1 by its claim pin, and the plan size rule (`PLAN_SIZE_FROM`) reads the pin at the plan's baseline commit. The version 2 review record, in force since 0.3.1, is not changed by this release. A ticket claimed under 0.4.0 whose approach critique begins after the upgrade therefore keeps five rounds and its stop but is held to the round 2 grounds rule; one whose record began before the upgrade keeps the old grounds rule.
- Lane R, research (new, no cut-off constant: it applies only to a ticket written as lane R): a ticket that asks a question accord cannot answer from the code, and asks for no change, is worked in lane R. `accord new ticket <id> --lane R` writes its template with `## Question`, `## Timebox` (`<n> minutes` or `<n> hours`) and `## Token budget` (`<n>`, `<n>k` or `<n>M` `weighted tokens`). The owner sets the timebox and the token budget; the session proposes neither. `accord gate ready` passes when the three sections are written and `## Decisions` quotes the owner on both values (D-13); `ac_hash` is taken over the three sections. Lane R has no approach, plan, critic, code review or verification: `accord gate plan` refuses an R ticket as having no plan (`gate.lane-r-plan`), and `accord round`, `accord approve`, `accord review-package`, `accord brief` and `accord task-done` refuse it with exit 2. An epic is never lane R.
- `accord budget <id>` (new): prints `within budget` (exit 0) or `stop: ask owner` (exit 1) for a lane R ticket, the elapsed time from the committer time of the ticket's `claim: <id>` commit, the weighted tokens of the checkout's own Claude Code transcripts since that claim (fresh input 1, cache write 1.25, cache read 0.1, output 5, each message once), and the `Spent:` line the research record copies. It stops only strictly past the timebox or the budget, and then prints the question for the owner and how the answer is written. Tokens read `not measured` when no full count since the claim exists: the checkout has no transcript folder, or its earliest transcript line is later than the claim (Claude Code deletes transcripts after 30 days by default); then the time alone decides (D-20). A ticket of another lane, an allowance that cannot be read, or a ticket with no claim commit is a usage error (exit 2).
- Lane R Done: the session writes a record under `accord/research/` with its header and a `Spent:` line, and lists it under `research:` in the ticket. `accord gate done` passes an R ticket when `ac_hash` still matches the three sections, `## Decisions` still quotes the owner's values, a listed record that the branch added or changed after the ticket's claim commit holds a `Spent:` line, and the branch changes nothing under `packages/`. A listed record that already existed is evidence: allowed, but never the ticket's result, and its `Spent:` line is not read; when git cannot say which records changed, or the ticket has no claim commit, no record counts. The timebox is compared with the larger of the record's `Spent:` time and the time git measures from the claim commit to the record's last change (the clock `accord budget` reads), so a `Spent:` line written lower decides nothing; the tokens cannot be measured from git and are read as written. A record past the ticket's timebox or token budget is refused unless it holds the owner's `Owner: write now — "…"` or `Owner: extended to … — "…"` line; an extension is the new value written into the ticket with its quoted Decision and a fresh `accord gate ready` (D-14). It asks for no plan, review, verification, suite or ticks.
- Lane R in CI and the views: the CI workflow `accord init` and `accord upgrade` write reads `ac_hash` on a lane R ticket as on lane S (`[SR]`), so a ready R ticket's accord-only pull request runs `accord gate done`, and an R ticket holding `ac_hash` cannot be deleted before it is dropped. A ticket approved as lane M, L or P that moves to lane R is refused as a lane drop, as a move to S is. `accord status`, `accord dashboard`, `accord anchor` and the session line show R tickets with their stage: `intent` until `gate ready`, `research` until a listed record the branch changed since the claim holds a `Spent:` line, then `done` (on the default branch once merged); the session line counts `research` as in progress. `accord status --json` may now carry the stage `research`, and `accord show --json` a `question` field (the question, with `intent` left empty) for an R ticket; `accord show` prints `Question:`. The `/accord` skill (a skill change shipped by `accord upgrade`) proposes lane R, without the scout, for a request that only asks a question, and follows the new `lane-r.md` from the owner's timebox and budget to the pull request.
- Lane R and the schema: `R` is a new value of the published ticket schema's `lane`, a one-way addition (D-10): a ticket that uses it needs this release or later, and an older accord refuses it. Tickets in flight and repositories pinned to an older accord change nothing until they write an R ticket.
- Lane R known limits: the no-code check of `accord gate done` covers `packages/` only, as D-10 says, so in a repository that keeps its code elsewhere, or for root files such as `package.json` and `.github/workflows/`, an R branch's code change is not refused; the timebox is calendar time since the claim commit, so nights and weekends count, and the owner's answer line (`Owner: write now` or `Owner: extended to`) covers a ticket that ran past it; no accord command brings the default branch into a lane R branch that is behind it (`accord worktree merge` runs only at verify and refuses R), so such a pull request is merged as GitHub allows; tokens read `not measured` when the ticket's session ran in another checkout, such as the main one, because only the checkout's own transcripts are read.
